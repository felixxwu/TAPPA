### My hypothesis was wrong, and the grader's replacement is better

I went in believing: *what is enumerated most explicitly gets done; what is merely
mentioned gets dropped.* I asked a grader to attack it. It did, and I accept the
refutation:

- **T003 falsifies the strong form outright.** Reachability was item **2 of an
  explicitly numbered four-part list**, at the edit site, with four lines of rationale
  — the *most* enumerated item in that note. It was still dropped. What changed between
  rounds 007 and 008 was not salience: I removed the cheap way to satisfy it
  (retagging) and left only an expensive one (authoring a rally in another file).
- **T004's docs were not actually done.** Files-touched says "test → docs"; correctness
  says the test was dropped, all three docs were written **wrong**, and the
  anti-duplication rule in `features/hud.md` was deleted. The probe did the cheap,
  unverifiable thing (plausible prose) and skipped the expensive, verifiable one.
- **Where a machine gate existed, it worked.** `test_region_docs.gd` shipped in round
  007; in round 008 the region doc came out correct while the HUD docs — same probe
  class, same round, same prose task — came out wrong. One had a test behind it, the
  other a comment.

**The accurate generalisation: these agents complete what is cheap and stop at their
own model of "done". Enumeration is roughly orthogonal.** The grader also offered an
alternative I had not considered and cannot rule out: I keep re-scoping the task
between rounds, so both see-saws may just be "the probe spends its budget on the two
cheapest of three obligations, and I keep changing which one is cheap."
